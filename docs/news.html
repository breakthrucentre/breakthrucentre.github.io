Our story in the news

Thirty-six years of newspaper clippings, magazine pages and photos from our scrapbook. Each one tells part of the story of a little Kidz Club that grew into Breakthrough Centre.

The early years

Our first newspaper story · 1999

Fresh air and fun keeps kids away from crime

Our very first media coverage. Every Saturday from 2 to 4pm, 80 to 90 children met at the Radha Roopsingh School for praise and worship, games, puppetry, clowning, drama and a lesson. Jessica had started the club to keep local children away from crime and drugs, and Ps Trevor had just begun music classes, teaching the children to sing and play guitar. To buy Christmas gifts, the two of them sold children's books and stationery, and did clowning and puppet shows at birthday parties.

"Race is not an issue for us."

From our scrapbook · 2000

Etete Millennium Party

Etete welcomed the new millennium with a party, and the children's message to everyone who came was simply: WELCOME.

The North Coast Courier · 30 November 2001

The magic of Christmas

1,500 children came to our Christmas party, up from 900 the year before. Every child received a gift, donated by the parents of Umhlali Primary, and a hot meal, donated by Quick Plumbers in Shakaskraal. The club had 200 full-time members and had just opened a second branch in Shakaskraal, and the Umhlali police helped bring those children to Etete so that everyone could join in.

Our fight against crime and poverty · 2002

Kids Club shines a little light

Between 100 and 250 children, from toddlers to 18-year-olds, were coming every Saturday to sing, play and learn. We ran a feeding programme twice a month with help from our church and local businesses, and children who had been with us for five years or more had become assistants, teaching the little ones about abuse and other issues.

Christmas party 2005 · 2005

Santa visits Etete

Over 1,300 children and 200 parents came to our Christmas party at Radha Roopsingh Primary. The children went home with toys and party packs, and parents received food hampers of rice, flour, beans and dhall.

"In the past 15 years, we have seen many children grow into successful people."

Heroes, ballet and a growing centre

The North Coast Courier · 31 March 2006

Shakaskraal couple win national heroes awards

Radha Roopsingh Primary teacher Simi Persad nominated Ps Trevor and Jessica as "national heroes", and they won the Lubners INHOUSE Club Heroes Award, chosen from entries across South Africa. The R10 000 prize went towards building the centre.

INHOUSE Club magazine · 2006

Lighting up Etete

Our story in a national magazine. It told how Jessica started Kidz Club in the garden of the home she grew up in, and how the little blue tin house had become a preschool, crèche, community centre and church used by about 200 young people every week. Jessica's mother donated the house to be used as a community centre.

"The charm of the little, blue corrugated iron house"

The North Coast Courier · 22 June 2007

"Never give up your dreams"

A profile of Jessica: mother, wife, schoolteacher, counsellor and co-founder. She started Kidz Club on 10 October 1990. After she married Ps Trevor in 1994 they moved away, and her sister Shavera kept the club going until they came back in 1999 and took it over again. In 2002 the club was registered as a non-profit organisation.

"Dreams are not born without sacrifice, dedication and commitment."

The North Coast Courier · 5 October 2007

Surprise celebration for community centre founder

The children and our church family threw Ps Trevor a surprise 40th birthday party at the centre, with drums, dancers and speeches about how he and the centre had changed their lives.

The North Coast Courier · 19 October 2007

Ballerinas in the making

Ten little girls from Etete were learning ballet, thanks to Pam Mills of Ballito. Pam began by teaching line dancing, asked the girls one afternoon if they would like to learn ballet, and they never looked back.

The North Coast Courier · 30 November 2007

Happy faces this Christmas

More than 500 children came to our Christmas party at Radha Roopsingh Primary. Christian group Spin the Flava entertained them, and everyone had breyani for lunch.

The North Coast Courier · 21 December 2007

Day care creche and graduation

Our preschool, then called Daddy's Day Care and Creche, held its Grade R graduation and a sports day with a tug-of-war. Teacher Eunice Thabede stood proudly with her graduates.

The North Coast Courier · 4 July 2008

Youth day talent show

Our first Youth Day rally drew about 170 young people, who showed off raps, art, modelling, poetry, singing and dancing. We used the day to launch our Youth Club for 12 to 21-year-olds, meeting every Saturday.

North Coast Times · 1 August 2008

Caught in the spirit of the Kidz Club

Thiasha Naidoo, then a Grade 10 learner from Shakaskraal, volunteered at Kidz Club for her community project. Today she is Dr Thiasha Naidoo.

Stanger Weekly · 5 September 2008

Community centre wins Heroes award

We won R10 000 in the Nedbank Heroes competition, our second award for community work. The cheque was handed over at the centre by the Nedbank Ballito manager. The money was for sports equipment and indoor games, with the rest going to our building fund.

From our scrapbook · 2009

Giving the kids a chance

With the Nedbank prize we bought table tennis, a pool table, a table-top soccer machine, board games and soccer balls. We also opened a youth help desk for young people facing issues such as teenage pregnancy, drugs, abuse and peer pressure.

"Children in the area can still do what they love but in a controlled environment"

Christmas, sponsors and empowerment

From our scrapbook

Kidz Club Christmas fun in Nkobongo

More than 900 children in Nkobongo received early Christmas presents at the Nkobongo community hall, after a party in Etete for over 1,500 children earlier that December.

From our scrapbook

Kidz Club Christmas Party

KwaDukuza Standard Bank branch manager John Lynch handed over toys for our annual Christmas party at Radha Roopsingh School.

Stanger Weekly

Festive food hampers

Disadvantaged families were treated to a meal, clothes and a food hamper on Christmas Day, so that they could enjoy the festive season too.

From our scrapbook

Ho ho ho

Over 300 children from disadvantaged homes came to our annual Christmas party, with face painting (Spiderman was the favourite), a puppet show about preventing child abuse, dancing and games.

"We want every child to experience the joy Christmas brings."

From our scrapbook

Free sewing classes offered

Women from Etete took a free three-month sewing course at the centre every day. Siza Water donated five sewing machines, an overlocker and two irons, and Stanger Waste Centre sponsored the material. Teacher Timna Tweedie ran the classes.

From our scrapbook

Waffles, ice-cream and syrup

Many of our children had never had a waffle, so our volunteers made waffles with ice cream and syrup for the whole Kidz Club.

From our scrapbook

Ballerinas perform for local clubs

Our ballet girls put on a performance for the Wednesday Club and the Sugarland Book Club, who sponsor their classes. By then Pam Mills had been teaching ballet as a volunteer for three years.

From our scrapbook

Usha struts her stuff

Mrs India South Africa contestant Usha Hemraj chose our community centre as her charity, and the proceeds of her dinner and dance at the Durban City Hall came to the centre.

Breakthrough Centre and the Academy

From our scrapbook · 21 March

Human Rights Day celebrated in writing

Eight local schools came to Breakthrough Centre for our Human Rights Day art and poetry writing contest: Groutville High, Sizani Combined Primary, Tinley Manor Primary, Radha Roopsingh Primary, Shakaskraal Primary, Shakaskraal Secondary, Nonhlevu Secondary and Shakaskraal S.A. Primary. Highway Radio's Phindile Phewa and artist Shavera Sayed judged.

From our scrapbook · 29 November 2014

Talent in torrents

At the Breakthrough Centre Academy Concert and Graduation, learners from 2 to 6 years old performed dances, sketches, songs and recitals. Our Grade R class of 2014 graduated in style.
